📅 D-Day Calculator

Count down or up to any date, with the day of the week.

What is a D-Day Calculator?

The D-Day calculator counts the days remaining until, or elapsed since, a specific date. It is used for exam dates, anniversaries, deadlines, and countdowns.

How it's calculated

The count is the target date minus today's date. If today is the target date, it shows D-Day; afterward, D+n; before, D-n.

Where does the term D-Day come from?

It is military terminology. Rather than naming a specific date for an operation, which could compromise security, planners simply referred to it as D-Day, using D for Day. Time was handled the same way with H-Hour. The most famous D-Day is June 6, 1944 (the Normandy landings), but the term was originally a generic placeholder used for any operation, not a proper noun.

Good to know

  • D-Day itself is not D-0, it is just D-Day. The day before is D-1.
  • Countdown culture is especially strong in some countries, where major exam dates are widely tracked with a running D-Day counter for months in advance.
  • From January 1 to January 3 spans 3 calendar days, but the date difference is only 2 — the source of confusion around trip lengths like 2 nights, 3 days.
  • Leap year rules have three layers: divisible by 4 is a leap year, but divisible by 100 is not, unless it is also divisible by 400.
  • Milestone counting (100 days, 1000 days) usually counts the start date as day 1, which is one day different from a simple date subtraction.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Is today day 1 or day 0?

It depends on the context. D-Day notation treats today as D-Day (0), while anniversaries typically count the starting day as day 1.

Q. How do I calculate D+100?

Add 100 days to the reference date. Anniversary conventions that count the start date as day 1 would actually add 99 days instead.

Q. Is '3 days left' the same as '3 nights'?

If 3 days remain, you will sleep 3 more nights, but the date difference is 3 days and the span (inclusive of today) is 4 days.
