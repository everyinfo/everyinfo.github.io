🎯 Percent Calculator

Calculate percentages, ratios, percent change, and discounted prices.

What is a Percentage Calculator?

The percentage calculator computes proportions, increase/decrease rates, and discounted prices — answering questions like 'What percent of B is A?' or 'What is the result of a percent change?'

How it's calculated

Percentage = part ÷ whole × 100
Percent change = (new value − old value) ÷ old value × 100
The denominator for percent change is always the original value.

Percent vs. percentage point

If approval rating rises from 40% to 44%, that is a 4 percentage point (pp) increase and also a 10% increase. Both are correct, but they describe very different magnitudes. That is why financial and statistical reporting always distinguishes 'pp' from '%'.

Good to know

  • A 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease does not return to the original. 100 → 150 → 75. This is why a stock that drops 50% needs a 100% gain to fully recover.
  • The percent sign (%) evolved from a handwritten abbreviation of the Italian per cento (per hundred).
  • Per mille (‰) is parts-per-thousand, used for things like blood alcohol concentration or ocean salinity.
  • Stacked discounts do not simply add. A 30% discount followed by an additional 20% is not 50% off — it is actually 44% off (100 → 70 → 56).
  • The same trap applies to sales tax: subtracting a flat percentage from a tax-included total gives the wrong number — you have to divide, not subtract.

Frequently asked questions

Q. What is the total discount for 30% off followed by an additional 20% off?

44%. The math is 100 × 0.7 × 0.8 = 56, so you pay 56% of the original price.

Q. How many times larger is a 100% increase?

Double. A 200% increase means 3 times the original, and 300% means 4 times.

Q. When should I use % versus percentage points (pp)?

Use pp for the difference between two percentages, and % for the relative change from an original value.
