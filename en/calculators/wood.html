Calculate lumber volume in board-feet (or m³/jae) and total cost, in USD or KRW.

What is a Lumber Volume Calculator?

The lumber volume calculator computes the volume of wood from its width, thickness, length, and quantity, converting between board feet, cubic meters, and other units used in the timber trade.

How it's calculated

1 board foot = 1 in × 12 in × 12 in ≈ 0.00236 m³. This is the standard unit for North American lumber trade.

Worked example

Example: 10 pieces of lumber, 2in × 8in × 12ft (nominal)

  • Board feet per piece: (2×8×12)÷12 = 16 board feet
  • Total for 10 pieces: 160 board feet ≈ 0.378 m³ (160×0.00236)

Real-world sources of error

  • Moisture content: Green (freshly cut) lumber can weigh nearly twice as much as kiln-dried lumber of the same volume, which matters for shipping weight estimates.
  • Milling loss: Converting a log into finished lumber loses material to sawdust and trim, so raw log volume doesn't equal usable board footage.

Why lumber sizes do not match their labels

A North American 2×4 is not actually 2 inches by 4 inches — its actual finished size is about 1.5 in × 3.5 in (38mm × 89mm). The label refers to the nominal (rough-cut) size before drying and planing, not the actual finished dimension. This mismatch between labeled and measured size is completely standard and expected in the lumber trade.

Good to know

  • Freshly cut (green) lumber can be nearly half water by weight, so it is significantly heavier than kiln-dried lumber of the same volume — a detail that matters a lot for shipping cost estimates.
  • Board foot (1 in × 12 in × 12 in ≈ 0.00236 m³) is the standard North American trade unit, while several Asian markets historically used comparable pre-metric volume units — both are examples of trade conventions that persist because entire pricing systems were built around them.
  • Wood strength depends heavily on grain direction. The same cross-section oriented differently can have dramatically different resistance to bending — orientation is effectively part of the structural spec.
  • Nominal vs. actual dimensions is one of the most common points of confusion for first-time lumber buyers, especially those used to metric, dimension-matches-label conventions.
  • Structural lumber grading (softwood grades, moisture content classes) significantly affects allowable load, which is why architectural drawings specify grade, not just dimensions.

Frequently asked questions

Q. What is a board foot?

A board foot is 1 inch by 12 inches by 12 inches, about 0.00236 cubic meters. It is the standard unit for lumber pricing in North America.

Q. Why is a 2x4 not actually 2 by 4 inches?

That is the nominal (rough-cut) size before drying and planing. The actual finished size is smaller, typically about 1.5 in by 3.5 in.

Q. How do I convert board feet to cubic meters?

Multiply board feet by approximately 0.00236 to get cubic meters.
