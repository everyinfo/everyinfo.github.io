🍰 Fraction Calculator

Add, subtract, multiply, or divide fractions with visual pie/bar diagrams and step-by-step solutions.

What is a Fraction Calculator?

This calculator performs fraction arithmetic and shows the common-denominator steps.

How it's calculated

Addition and subtraction require converting to a common denominator (using the LCM) before combining numerators. Multiplication multiplies numerators and denominators directly. Division multiplies by the reciprocal.

Step-by-step example

Adding 1/4 + 1/6

  • Step 1 Find a common denominator (LCM): LCM of 4 and 6 = 12
  • Step 2 Convert: 1/4 = 3/12, 1/6 = 2/12
  • Step 3 Add: 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12

Interpretation: 5 and 12 share no common factors, so this is already in lowest terms.

Why does division flip the fraction?

Dividing by ½ is really asking 'how many ½'s fit into this number?' Three whole units contain six halves, so 3 ÷ ½ = 6, which is the same as 3 × 2. This is exactly why dividing by a number less than 1 makes the result bigger — you're counting how many smaller pieces fit inside.

Good to know

  • A fraction is really just a frozen division symbol. The horizontal fraction bar literally means division, and the ÷ symbol itself is thought to derive from a fraction's shape (a bar with a dot above and below).
  • Ancient Egyptians used only unit fractions (numerator of 1) — they expressed 3/4 as 1/2 + 1/4, a cumbersome system that persisted for over 3,000 years.
  • Division by zero is undefined for exactly the reason fractions make clear: 6 ÷ 0 asks 'how many zeros add up to 6,' and no amount of zero, added any number of times, ever reaches 6.
  • Musical time signatures are literally fractions — a quarter note is 1/4 the length of a whole note, and notations like 3/4 or 6/8 time are fractions describing rhythm.
  • Computers can't represent 0.1 exactly — in binary, 1/10 becomes an infinite repeating fraction, which is why 0.1 + 0.2 famously computes as 0.30000000000000004 rather than exactly 0.3. Financial calculations often use fractions or integers instead of decimals to avoid this.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Why do fractions need a common denominator?

Because pieces of different sizes can't be added directly — a common denominator makes all the pieces the same size first.

Q. How do I reduce a fraction to lowest terms?

Divide both numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor.

Q. How do I handle mixed numbers?

Convert to an improper fraction first, do the calculation, then convert the result back to a mixed number if needed.
