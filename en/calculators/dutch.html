🧾 Bill Split Calculator

Split a bill evenly among any number of people, with rounding options.

What is a Bill Splitting Calculator?

The bill splitting calculator divides a total amount among a group of people, and can also calculate settlement differences when people paid unequal amounts.

How it's calculated

Amount per person = total divided by number of people. When the total does not divide evenly, the difference is usually rounded up and absorbed by whoever pays, or carried over to the next outing.

Why 'going Dutch'?

The phrase comes from historical Anglo-Dutch rivalry in the 17th and 18th centuries, when English picked up several unflattering idioms about the Dutch (Dutch courage, Dutch uncle, and going Dutch among them) during a period of intense trade competition. What began as a slight has since become a completely neutral, widely accepted way to describe splitting costs fairly.

Good to know

  • Splitting norms differ by country. The US commonly splits by what each person actually ordered (separate checks), while many European countries default to an even split of the total.
  • Settlement apps use a minimum-transfer algorithm: even with many people owing each other money in different directions, netting the debts can dramatically reduce the number of transfers needed.
  • Splitting down to the last cent is impractical, so rounding to a convenient unit is standard practice, with the remainder typically absorbed by whoever paid.
  • In mixed groups where some people did not drink alcohol, splitting the alcohol cost separately from the rest of the bill tends to reduce friction.
  • The phrase 'Dutch treat' is closely related but slightly older, originally meaning each person pays their own way rather than one person treating the group.

Frequently asked questions

Q. What if the total doesn't divide evenly?

It is common to round up to a convenient unit and let the person who paid keep the small remainder, or carry it to the next outing.

Q. Is an even split fair if people ordered different amounts?

If the amounts differ significantly, splitting by item is more equitable. For small differences, an even split is simpler and usually accepted.

Q. How do multiple people who owe each other settle up efficiently?

Calculate each person's net balance first, then net the debts against each other to minimize the number of transfers.
