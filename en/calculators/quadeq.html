📊 Quadratic Equation Calculator

Solve ax²+bx+c=0 using the discriminant and quadratic formula.

What is a Quadratic Equation Calculator?

This calculator solves ax² + bx + c = 0 and shows the discriminant.

How it's calculated

Quadratic formula: x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) ÷ 2a
Discriminant D = b² − 4ac

Step-by-step example

Solving x² − 5x + 6 = 0

  • Step 1 Discriminant: D = (−5)² − 4(1)(6) = 25 − 24 = 1
  • Step 2 Since D>0, two distinct real roots: x = (5 ± 1) ÷ 2
  • Step 3 Solve: x = 6÷2 = 3 or x = 4÷2 = 2

Interpretation: Checking (x−2)(x−3)=x²−5x+6 confirms this matches the original equation.

The discriminant tells you everything at a glance

Without solving for the roots directly, the discriminant alone tells you how many solutions exist and what type. D > 0 means two distinct real roots, D = 0 means one repeated root, and D < 0 means no real roots (two complex roots instead). When the value under the square root is negative, there's no real-number answer — this is exactly where imaginary numbers (i) enter the picture.

Good to know

  • The quadratic formula is simply the result of completing the square. It's not something to memorize blindly — it's a derived conclusion you can reconstruct from first principles.
  • Babylonian clay tablets from around 2000 BCE already show methods for solving quadratic equations, though they only handled positive solutions since negative numbers weren't yet conceptualized.
  • Vieta's formulas let you find properties of the roots without solving explicitly: the sum of the roots equals −b/a, and their product equals c/a — exactly what you're doing when you factor by 'finding two numbers that add to b and multiply to c.'
  • Cubic and quartic equations also have general formulas, but they're extremely complex — and Galois and Abel proved that no general formula exists for degree 5 and higher, one of mathematics' most striking impossibility results.
  • Real-world problems involving maximum or minimum values — projectile trajectories, optimal pricing, braking distance — very often reduce to quadratic equations.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Does a negative discriminant mean there's no answer?

No real solutions exist, but two complex (imaginary) solutions do.

Q. Is there a way to solve without the quadratic formula?

If the expression factors easily, factoring is much faster. The formula is the fallback when it doesn't.

Q. What happens if a = 0?

The equation loses its quadratic term and becomes linear. A true quadratic equation requires a ≠ 0.
