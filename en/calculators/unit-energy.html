What is a Energy Unit Converter?

The energy unit converter switches between J, kJ, kcal, kWh, and BTU.

How it's calculated

1 kcal = 4.184 kJ, 1 kWh = 3,600 kJ = 860 kcal, 1 BTU ≈ 1.055 kJ

The same energy, different names

Every field invented its own convenient unit: J (joule) for physics and engineering, kcal for nutrition, kWh for electricity billing, and BTU for heating/cooling equipment (especially in the US). They're all measuring the same physical quantity. A bowl of rice (about 300 kcal) contains roughly 1.26 MJ of energy — the same as 0.35 kWh, or running a 60W bulb for about 6 hours.

Good to know

  • The ton of refrigeration (RT), still used on some US air conditioner specs (1 RT = 12,000 BTU/h), literally originates from melting one ton of ice in 24 hours, back when mechanical refrigeration was replacing ice-based cooling.
  • 1 kWh = 3,600 kJ follows straightforwardly from 1 kW × 3,600 seconds.
  • A person's daily energy use (about 2,000 kcal) is roughly 2.3 kWh — worth well under a dollar in electricity terms, though sourcing that energy from food costs considerably more.
  • The joule is named after James Joule, a beer brewer whose hobby experiments established that heat and mechanical work are equivalent, laying groundwork for the first law of thermodynamics.
  • BTU-based air conditioner ratings remain common in the US, while most of the world has standardized on kW for cooling capacity.

Frequently asked questions

Q. How many kcal is 1 kWh?

About 860 kcal.

Q. Is a calorie the same as a kilocalorie?

1 kcal = 1,000 cal. Food labels' 'Calories' (capital C) typically mean kilocalories.

Q. What is BTU used for?

Common in US-style heating and cooling equipment specs. 1 BTU is the heat needed to raise 1 pound of water by 1°F.
