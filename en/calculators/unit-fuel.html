What is a Fuel Economy Converter?

The fuel economy converter switches between km/L, L/100km, and mpg.

How it's calculated

L/100km = 100 ÷ (km/L)
1 US mpg = 0.425 km/L, 1 UK mpg = 0.354 km/L

km/L and L/100km move in opposite directions

km/L is better when higher; L/100km is better when lower — an inverted relationship that trips people up. Improving from 10 to 12 km/L and from 20 to 22 km/L both look like '+2' on that scale, but the actual fuel saved is 3.7x greater for the first case. That's exactly why L/100km, used across much of Europe, directly reflects real savings.

Good to know

  • Improving a poor-mileage vehicle saves more fuel than improving an already-efficient one. This counterintuitive effect is sometimes called the 'MPG illusion.'
  • US and UK mpg are not the same — because the gallon itself differs. The same car can read 30 mpg in the US and 36 mpg in the UK.
  • Official ratings are typically a combined cycle — a weighted average of city and highway driving — so your real mileage depends on how closely your driving matches that mix.
  • Electric vehicles use km/kWh or MPGe, where MPGe treats 33.7 kWh as energy-equivalent to one gallon of gasoline.
  • Hybrid vehicles often get better mileage in city driving due to regenerative braking, the opposite pattern from gas-only cars.

Frequently asked questions

Q. What is 12 km/L in L/100km?

About 8.3 L/100km. Calculate as 100 ÷ 12.

Q. Which country's mpg standard is being used?

Check the source — the US gallon (3.785 L) and UK gallon (4.546 L) are different sizes.

Q. What is combined fuel economy?

A weighted average of city and highway driving cycles, used as the official rating.
