What is a Cooking Measurement Converter?

The cooking measurement converter switches between tablespoons, teaspoons, cups, and milliliters — useful for matching recipe measurements to your kitchen tools.

How it's calculated

1 tablespoon (Tbsp) = 15 mL, 1 teaspoon (tsp) = 5 mL. Cup sizes vary by country — the US cup is about 240 mL, while some other countries use 200 mL or other standards.

Cup sizes differ by country

Using a 200 mL measuring cup for a US recipe calling for '1 cup' leaves you about 40 mL short. For baking, where ratios matter precisely, this gap can noticeably affect results — always check which country's cup standard a recipe is written in.

Good to know

  • '1 cup = 8 fl oz' is about volume, not weight. A cup of water weighs about 240g, but a cup of flour weighs only about 120g — because of air trapped between the particles.
  • That is exactly why serious baking recipes use a kitchen scale instead of cups — how tightly flour is packed into a cup can shift the amount by 30g or more.
  • A 'pinch' is informally treated as about 1/8 teaspoon (0.6 mL), though there's no official standard — it's a cooking convention, not a precise unit.
  • Liquid and dry measuring cups are shaped differently — liquid cups have headroom above the line for pouring without spilling, while dry cups are meant to be leveled off flat.
  • 1 tablespoon (15 mL) equals exactly 3 teaspoons (5 mL each) — handy to remember when a recipe only gives one unit.

Frequently asked questions

Q. How many mL is 1 tablespoon?

15 mL. Since 1 teaspoon is 5 mL, 1 tablespoon equals 3 teaspoons.

Q. Does it matter whether I use a US or metric cup?

Yes, especially for baking. US cups are about 240 mL, while some countries use a 200 mL standard — always check what your recipe assumes.

Q. What's the difference between liquid and dry measuring cups?

Liquid cups have extra headroom for pouring without spilling; dry cups are designed to be leveled flat across the top for accuracy.
