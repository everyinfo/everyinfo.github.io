✈️ Volumetric Weight Calculator

Calculate volumetric weight and chargeable weight for air freight, courier, and sea LCL shipments.

What is a Air Freight Volumetric Weight Calculator?

The air freight volumetric weight calculator computes chargeable weight from a shipment's dimensions, comparing it against actual weight to determine the rate basis.

How it's calculated

Volumetric weight (kg) = length × width × height (cm) ÷ 6,000
Chargeable weight = max(actual weight, volumetric weight)
Some couriers divide by 5,000 instead — always check.

Why does light cargo still cost so much to fly?

An aircraft's real constraint is space, more than weight. So bulky, low-density cargo is priced by volume instead. The 6,000 divisor comes from treating 1 cubic meter as equivalent to 166.7 kg for pricing purposes — compared to ocean freight's 1,000 kg per CBM, air freight is about 6 times more sensitive to volume.

Good to know

  • Extra padding raises your bill. Thicker bubble wrap that bumps a box into the next size bracket increases volumetric weight even though actual weight hasn't changed.
  • Different couriers use different divisors. The IATA air freight standard is 6,000, but some international couriers use 5,000 instead.
  • Box measurements are typically rounded up, so a few extra millimeters can push a shipment into a more expensive size bracket.
  • Ocean and air freight use entirely different formulas — ocean uses CBM directly, air converts to kg.
  • Since chargeable weight is whichever is greater, reducing volume does nothing if actual weight is already the binding constraint.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Should I divide by 6,000 or 5,000?

The IATA air freight standard is 6,000. Some couriers use 5,000 under their own contract terms, so check your shipping quote.

Q. Does splitting a shipment into smaller boxes reduce cost?

Only if total volume decreases. Simply splitting a box often increases total volume due to extra wall thickness.

Q. What if volumetric weight is less than actual weight?

Then actual weight is used for pricing. Whichever value is larger always applies.
