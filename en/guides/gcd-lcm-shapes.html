GCD, LCM and the area and volume of shapes

How to tell greatest common divisor from least common multiple in word problems, area versus circumference of a circle, the missing ÷2 and ÷3, and cm² versus cm³.

Nov 26, 2026 · Elementary Math · 9 min read

GCD and LCM are rarely wrong in the arithmetic. They go wrong in the word problem, where the same numbers fit either one. Shapes go wrong the other way: the formula is known but the ÷2, ÷3 or the unit is dropped. This guide gives the sentence patterns that decide which one to use, and the places the factor disappears.

Divisors, multiples and primes form one chain

Divisors and multiples of 36

  • Divisors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36 → 9 of them
  • Prime factorisation: 36 = 2² × 3²
  • First ten multiples: 36, 72, 108, 144, 180, 216, 252, 288, 324, 360
  • A number with only 1 and itself as divisors is prime. 36 has nine, so it is not.

Divisors always come in pairs: (1, 36), (2, 18), (3, 12), (4, 9), (6, 6). Listing them that way prevents misses, and the calculator shows the pairs as a table. It accepts numbers up to 100,000.

GCD shares out, LCM meets again

12 and 18

  • 12 = 2² × 3, 18 = 2 × 3²
  • Common divisors: 1, 2, 3, 6 → GCD 6
  • LCM = 12 × 18 ÷ 6 = 36

12 sweets and 18 chocolates share equally among at most 6 people; buses every 12 and 18 days meet again on day 36.

Phrase in the problemWhat is askedExample
"share equally with none left", "largest number", "greatest number of people"GCD12 and 18 → 6
"smallest number", "at the same time", "meet again", "tile with no gaps"LCMevery 12 and 18 days → 36
"how many in each group", "cut into equal lengths"GCD12 m and 18 m → 6 m pieces
"smallest square that fits the rectangle exactly"LCM12 × 18 → 36 × 36

Equal sharing is always GCD; meeting again or filling exactly is always LCM. Two phrases settle the whole category of word problems.

Two identical triangles make a parallelogram; two identical trapezoids turned over also make one. That is where the ÷2 comes from in both cases.

Circle area and circumference are different quantities

Area = π r²  (cm²)  |  Circumference = 2πr  (cm)
  • Radius 5 cm → area = π × 25 = 78.54 cm²
  • Same circle, circumference = 2 × π × 5 = 31.42 cm
  • If the diameter is given, halve it first: diameter 10 cm → radius 5 cm

Area is in cm² (squared), circumference in cm (length). Different units mean the two cannot be added or compared.

"Diameter × 3.14" for area is off by a factor of four: a 10 cm circle is 78.54 cm², not 31.4. Finding the radius first is the habit that prevents it.

In the cone surface area l is the slant height: with r 3 and h 4, l = √(3²+4²) = 5. A radius-3 sphere gives 113.10 for both, but the units are cm³ and cm².

Top 6 things people get wrong

#MisconceptionCorrect basis
1Swapping GCD and LCM in a word problem"Share equally, largest" is GCD; "meet again, fill exactly, smallest" is LCM.
2Area of a circle as diameter × 3.14Area is πr². Forgetting to halve the diameter gives four times too little.
3Dropping ÷2 for triangles and trapezoidsBoth are half of a parallelogram made from two copies, so the half is always there.
4Dropping ÷3 for cones and pyramidsThey hold one third of the prism with the same base and height.
5Mixing cm² and cm³Area is squared, volume is cubed. Different units cannot be added.
6LCM as the product of the two numbersOnly when they are coprime. 12 × 18 = 216, but LCM is 36.

Practical notes

  • Read the working, not just the answer. The GCD/LCM calculator shows prime factorisation, common divisors and the product-over-GCD step. Checking only the answer hides the mistake.
  • Write the unit first. Decide cm² or cm³ before filling in numbers; it catches most slips.
  • Estimate before computing. A triangle inside an 8×5 rectangle cannot exceed 40. Comparing with the estimate exposes a missing ÷2 immediately.
  • Anchor on 1 L = 1,000 cm³. It is the most useful single line for volume conversions.

Frequently asked

What if the GCD is 1?

The numbers are coprime. Then the LCM equals their product: 8 and 9 give GCD 1 and LCM 72.

Is there a fast way to count divisors?

Add one to each exponent in the prime factorisation and multiply. 36 = 2² × 3² gives (2+1)(2+1) = 9.

Why does cylinder surface area count the base twice?

A closed cylinder has a lid and a base, hence 2πr². An open tank needs only πr² once.

Is it a coincidence that a radius-3 sphere has the same volume and area number?

4/3π×27 = 36π and 4π×9 = 36π, so the numbers match, but one is cm³ and the other cm². They are not the same quantity.
