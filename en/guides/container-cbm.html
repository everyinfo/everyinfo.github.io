20ft, 40ft and 40HQ internal dimensions, and how to stop CBM drifting

Internal dimensions and nominal CBM for six container types, box counts when floor loading, the 40HQ vs 40DV difference, LCL revenue tons and five ways to keep CBM accurate.

Nov 25, 2026 · Logistics & Trade · 7 min read

CBM is a carton's volume in cubic metres and the starting point for LCL freight and container planning. When the number from a calculator and the forwarder's figure differ by 5~10%, the cause is almost always which dimension was measured. This guide covers the real internal dimensions behind the CalcLex presets, and how to close the gap.

CBM = L × W × H ÷ 1,000,000,000 in millimetres. 20ft: 5,898 × 2,352 × 2,393 = 33.19 ㎥, matching the 33.2 listed.

A 40ft reefer loses more than 10 ㎥ to thicker walls and the refrigeration unit: 76.3 down to 66.0. If the cargo is not temperature controlled, that is reason enough not to book a reefer.

The CBM formula and its units

Volume of one carton

CBM = length(m) × width(m) × height(m) = L × W × H ÷ 1,000,000,000 (mm)
  • 600 × 400 × 300mm → 0.6 × 0.4 × 0.3 = 0.072 CBM
  • 100 cartons → 0.072 × 100 = 7.2 CBM
  • In litres 0.072 CBM = 72 L; in cubic feet 0.072 × 35.315 = 2.54 ft³

The calculator accepts mm, cm, m or inches and converts internally, and can show the result in ㎥, L or ft³. Leave the quantity blank and it calculates a single carton.

Theoretical maximum. Plan on 85~90% of this to leave room for handling, dunnage and load distribution.

Note that 40ft and 40HQ take the same 210 cartons per layer. The extra 305mm of height buys a tenth layer — 210 more cartons, 6.3 CBM. Not a whole container's worth, but the answer when those last 210 cartons are the problem.

40HQ versus 40DV: what 305mm buys

  • Volume: 2,698 − 2,393 = 305mm → 12.032 × 2.352 × 0.305 = about 8.6 CBM
  • Double-stacked pallets: a 1,300mm loaded pallet doubles to 2,600mm, which fits a 40HQ (2,698mm) but not a 40DV (2,393mm). For two-tier pallet loading, HQ is effectively required.
  • Freight: HQ usually costs slightly more per container, but often less per CBM.

Switch the calculator to 40HQ and press the double-stack button: if the tier count drops back to one, height is your limit.

LCL is billed on the larger of CBM and weight

Sea LCL charges a revenue ton (R/T): the greater of the volume in CBM and the weight in metric tons (1,000kg = 1 W/T).

CargoCBMWeightW/TBilled R/T
Light apparel, toys12.08,500 kg8.512.0 (volume)
Heavy machine parts9.012,000 kg12.012.0 (weight)

Both are 12 R/T for different reasons. For dense cargo, shaving CBM changes nothing; for light cargo, cutting weight changes nothing. Know which one you are paying on before optimising the wrong side.

Five ways to keep CBM accurate

  1. Use internal, not external, dimensions. Calculating from the 6,058mm external length of a 20ft container inflates CBM by more than 2%. The presets are internal.
  2. Measure the outside of the carton. Board thickness, tape and wrap all count. Entering 3~5mm under the real size shifts 0.1~0.2 CBM across 1,000 cartons.
  3. Check the quantity unit. The calculator counts cartons. If you buy by pallet, multiply cartons per pallet first.
  4. Assume 85% loading. That is the efficiency the CBM calculator applies to container estimates. You cannot put 33.2 CBM into a 33.2 CBM container.
  5. Check the door and the corrugation. Door width is slightly narrower than the internal width (around 2,340mm on standard boxes, varying by maker and age), and side corrugation takes 20~30mm at the edges. For large pallets or long material, check the door first.

Frequently asked

Is one 40HQ better than two 20fts?

On volume, two 20fts give 66.4 CBM against 76.3 for one 40HQ. The HQ carries nearly 10 CBM more with one set of freight and handling costs. For heavy cargo the two 20fts can win: 28,200kg payload each.

Should pallet volume be included in CBM?

Yes, include it. The pallet itself and the gaps between pallets both take space. For palletised cargo, get the pallet count from the container loading calculator first, then check the leftover space in CBM.

How full can a container really be?

85~90% is a realistic ceiling even with careful stacking. Beyond that there is no room to handle the cargo and damage risk rises. If the calculator shows more than 85% volume used, recheck dunnage and working space.
