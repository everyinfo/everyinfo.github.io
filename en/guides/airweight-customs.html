Air chargeable weight (C/W) and customs duty basics

How the greater of actual weight and volume weight becomes the chargeable weight, why air uses 6,000 and courier 5,000, how CIF builds the duty base, and how the $150 / $200 duty-free line is tested.

Nov 26, 2026 · Logistics & Trade · 9 min read

Air freight is billed by weight, but not always the weight on the scale. Light, bulky shipments — apparel, toys, cushioning — get billed on a weight converted from volume. This guide follows the exact order the calculator uses, then walks through the customs base, where the most expensive mistakes happen.

Chargeable weight is the greater of two weights

Three weights matter, and the calculator handles them in this order.

TermMeaningFormulaExample (60×50×40cm, 15kg, 1 box)
Gross weight G/WWeight on the scalekg per box × quantity15.0 kg
Volume weight V/WVolume converted to weightL×W×H (cm³) ÷ 6,00020.0 kg
Chargeable weight C/WThe weight you pay formax(G/W, V/W), rounded up to 0.5 kg20.0 kg

When volume weight is larger it wins, when gross weight is larger it wins. The two are never added together.

V/W = L × W × H × quantity ÷ 6,000  (air) C/W = max(G/W, V/W) → round up to the next 0.5 kg
  • 60 × 50 × 40 cm = 120,000 cm³
  • 120,000 ÷ 6,000 = 20.0 kg volume weight
  • Gross 15.0 kg < 20.0 kg → chargeable 20.0 kg

The same box by courier (÷5,000) bills 24.0 kg — 4 kg, or 20%, more.

6,000 versus 5,000 is a 20% gap

General air cargo treats 6,000 cm³ as one kilogram; international express carriers such as DHL and FedEx commonly use 5,000 cm³. A smaller divisor makes volume weight larger, so the same carton bills heavier by express.

ItemAir (÷6,000)Express (÷5,000)
Volume that equals 1 kg6,000 cm³5,000 cm³
1 CBM (= 1,000,000 cm³)166.7 kg200.0 kg
Volume weight, 60×50×40 cm20.0 kg24.0 kg
Chargeable weight at 15 kg actual20.0 kg24.0 kg

Remember it as: one cubic metre of air freight starts at 166.7 kg, by express at 200 kg.

For dense cargo the divisor changes nothing. A 50×40×30 cm carton (60,000 cm³) at 12.5 kg gives 10.0 kg volume weight by air and 12.0 kg by express — gross weight wins in both cases. The gap only appears on bulky freight, not automatically on every express shipment.

Rounding to 0.5 kg is billable too

The calculator rounds chargeable weight up to the next 0.5 kg (multiply by 2, round up, divide by 2). A 100×100×100 cm box has 166.67 kg of volume weight and bills as 167.0 kg. A third of a kilogram sounds trivial until it repeats across hundreds of cartons. Some carriers round to 1 kg or use 0.1 kg steps, so check the tariff condition.

Sea LCL prices the larger of CBM and weight ton

LCL sea freight uses the revenue ton (R/T): whichever is larger, the volume in CBM or the weight in tonnes (W/T, 1,000 kg). One 60×50×40 cm carton is 0.06 CBM and 0.0125 tonnes, so 0.06 R/T applies on volume. Different name, same principle as air: the billing unit is the larger of volume and weight.

Duty is charged on CIF, and VAT is charged on duty too

The customs value is goods + international freight + insurance, or CIF. Multiply that by the tariff rate to get duty, then add 10% VAT on (customs value + duty). Because VAT sits on top of duty, a 13% tariff item actually costs about 24.3%.

The calculator carries apparel and footwear at 13%, bags 8%, cosmetics 6.5%, health supplements 8%, toys 8%, electronics 8%, computers and phones 0%, books 0%, green coffee 2%. The real rate follows the 10-digit HS code.

The duty-free test uses the goods price; the duty base does not

This is the most common confusion. The low-value clearance limit is tested on the goods price only, with freight and insurance excluded. But once the shipment is taxable, the base becomes CIF, which includes freight and insurance. The same $20 of freight sits outside the test and inside the base.

OriginLimitGoods $180 · freight $20 · duty 8%
General$150Taxable — 51,888 KRW on a CIF base of 276,000 KRW
United States$200Duty-free low-value clearance

The origin selector (general $150 / US $200) drives that limit. $180 passes the US line and fails the general one.

The freight effect is easy to quantify: goods $160 plus $20 freight gives CIF $180. At 1,380 KRW that raises the base by 27,600 KRW and, at 8% duty, the tax bill by 3,588 KRW. When a quote "did not include the shipping in the tax", that is the gap.

Practical notes

  • Attack the volume first. If volume weight sets the charge, a smaller carton or vacuum compression removes billable kilos far more reliably than negotiating the rate.
  • Ask the forwarder which divisor applies. Some lanes mix 6,000 and 5,000. Knowing the written basis explains quote differences.
  • Confirm the HS code for duty. The calculator list holds representative rates. Use the customs service or a broker for the final figure, and check whether an FTA rate applies.
  • Use the customs exchange rate. The rate field is the customs rate, which differs from your card's settlement rate.

Frequently asked

What if actual and volume weight are equal?

The calculator labels it as volume weight applied. The billed amount is identical, so the practical result does not change.

Is a heavy bill on a light parcel a loss?

The rate is applied to chargeable weight, so you pay for 5 kg that you did not put on the scale. Compare against LCL sea freight — 0.06 R/T in this example — before deciding the mode.

Why is the US limit different?

Shipments from the United States operate on a $200 low-value limit. Selecting United States in the origin field applies it.

If the tax is zero, is paperwork unnecessary?

Low-value clearance usually needs no import declaration. Items outside that scheme, or shipments above the limit, move to formal clearance and may require one.
