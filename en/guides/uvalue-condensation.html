U-value calculation and the insulation thickness that stops indoor condensation

The U-value formula with surface resistances, thermal conductivity by material, the insulation thickness each regional limit requires, and how dew point decides condensation.

Nov 25, 2026 · Design & Build · 9 min read

U-value measures how much heat escapes through a wall, roof or floor. The unit is W/㎡·K and lower is better. This guide works through the exact formula the CalcLex calculator uses, shows how much insulation each regional limit demands, and explains how dew point decides whether a surface condenses.

One formula: add every resistance, then invert

U = 1 ÷ (Rsi + ΣR + Rse)   |   R = thickness(m) ÷ λ
  • Rsi, Rse: surface resistances inside and outside (㎡·K/W) — the still air film on each face.
  • R: one layer's resistance. Thicker layers and lower λ both raise it.
  • ΣR: the sum of all layers. Adding insulation adds exactly that R and lowers U.

At the same thickness XPS (0.027) insulates about 59 times better than concrete (1.6). That is the whole reason insulation exists.

Worked example: gypsum 9.5 + concrete 200 + XPS 100

The calculator's default build-up as an exterior wall

  • Surface: 0.11 + 0.043 = 0.153
  • Gypsum 9.5mm: 0.0095 ÷ 0.18 = 0.0528
  • Concrete 200mm: 0.200 ÷ 1.60 = 0.1250
  • XPS 100mm: 0.100 ÷ 0.027 = 3.7037
  • Total R = 4.034 ㎡·K/W

U = 1 ÷ 4.034 = 0.248 W/㎡·K · heat loss = 0.248 × 30㎡ × 20K = about 149W

Swap the XPS for EPS (λ 0.036) and R falls to 2.778, lifting U to 0.322 — about 30% more heat loss from boards that look identical.

Condensation is decided by dew point versus surface temperature

Warm indoor air meeting a cold surface condenses when that surface drops below the dew point. The calculator compares the two numbers.

An uninsulated concrete wall at 20℃ indoors and 60% humidity drops to 10.02℃ at the surface — below the 12.00℃ dew point, so water beads on corners and window reveals. Adding just 50mm of XPS lifts the surface to 18.49℃ and the condensation disappears.

What site work usually misses

  • Thermal bridges: a steel frame or slab penetrating the insulation can multiply the local U-value. The layer calculation assumes uniform layers, so bridge-break details are separate.
  • Inside versus outside insulation: internal insulation leaves the structure cold and prone to condensation; external insulation wraps it and reduces both bridging and condensation.
  • Corners and frames: flat areas can meet the limit while corners still condense first because their heat loss per surface area is higher.
  • Air tightness: good insulation with leaks still condenses wherever moist air reaches a cold surface. Tape and seal with the insulation.

Frequently asked

How is U different from λ?

λ describes one material; U describes the whole assembly, including every layer and both surface films. A low-λ board in a thin or bridged wall can still give a poor U.

Does doubling insulation halve U?

Almost, but not exactly. Surface and other layer resistances are fixed, so doubling only the insulation does not double total R. Going from XPS 100 (U 0.248) to 200 gives 0.129 — slightly better than half.

Which conditions should I check condensation at?

The worst case: coldest outdoor air with the highest indoor humidity you expect. Try 20℃ inside, −10℃ outside and 60% RH, and check the surface stays above the dew point.
