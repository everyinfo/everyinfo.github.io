Mold shrinkage in per-mille (‰): the formula and a resin reference table

What a per-mille shrink rate means, how to convert a mold size from a part size, reference shrinkage for ABS, PP, PC, POM and nine more resins, and what moves the number.

Nov 25, 2026 · Design & Build · 7 min read

The most common mistake in mold design is a unit error. The datasheet says 0.5%, the calculator field is in ‰ (per mille), and typing 0.5 makes the shrinkage ten times too small. This guide explains the unit, the formula, and reference values by resin.

‰ means one part in a thousand

Shrinkage is how much smaller the molded part ends up than the cavity it came from. % is one part in a hundred, ‰ is one part in a thousand, so 1% = 10‰.

Careful — the calculator's shrink field is per mille. Entering 0.5 for a 0.5% resin is read as 0.05%, and the mold barely grows. The calculator warns you when a value between 0 and 1 is entered.

The mold size formula

Common approximation (what the calculator uses)

Mold size = part size × (1 + shrinkage)

Exact equation, with shrinkage defined against the mold

Mold size = part size ÷ (1 − shrinkage)
  • Part 100mm, PC at 6‰ (0.6%) → 100 × 1.006 = 100.6mm
  • Exact: 100 ÷ 0.994 = 100.604mm — 0.004mm apart
  • Part 1,000mm at 2% (20‰) → 1,020mm vs 1,020.408mm — 0.408mm apart

Below 1% and under a few hundred millimetres the difference disappears. Above a metre with 2% shrinkage, 0.4mm can exceed an assembly tolerance. The calculator shows the same note under its result.

Grade, filler, colour and molding conditions all shift the real value. Treat this as a starting point and correct it with a trial shot for precision parts.

What moves the shrinkage

  • Wall thickness: thick sections cool late and shrink more. Large thickness differences cause local sink marks.
  • Mold temperature: a hot mold cools slowly, allowing crystallisation and more shrink; a cold mold shrinks less.
  • Packing pressure: with enough hold pressure the cavity stays full and shrink drops. A gate that freezes early blocks packing and increases shrink.
  • Fibre orientation: glass-filled resins shrink hardly at all along the fibre and much more across it. That is why PP-GF30 at 5‰ still varies by direction.
  • Moisture: nylons absorb water after molding and grow. Shrinkage alone does not describe that change.

Practical notes

  1. A mold can only be cut, not grown. A slightly oversized mold can be trimmed to size; an undersized one cannot be enlarged. When in doubt, err larger and adjust after the trial shot.
  2. Pick one datum. Applying shrinkage to every dimension compounds error away from the centre. Choose a centre or assembly datum and work from it.
  3. Trial shots are not optional. The calculator estimates; it does not approve a mold. Shoot once with the real resin and conditions, then measure.
  4. Re-set the value for regrind. Recycled PP is listed at 18‰ against 15‰ for virgin. With wide batch variation, check per lot.

Frequently asked

How do I enter 0.5%?

Multiply by ten and enter 5. The field is per mille: 0.5% = 5‰. Entering 0.5 calculates 0.05%.

Why does ABS shrink less than PP?

ABS is amorphous and PP is crystalline. Crystalline resins organise into crystals as they cool and lose more volume, so PP, HDPE and POM sit at 1.5~2% while ABS, PC and PS sit at 0.4~0.6%.

Is a bigger shrinkage value always safer?

No. Too large and the part overshoots its target; cutting back is possible but often means reworking features and gates. Start from the resin reference and narrow it with a trial shot.
